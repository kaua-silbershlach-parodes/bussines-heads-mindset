# Rules for multiplying wealth

![Rules for multiplying wealth](../images/026-Rules-for-multiplying-wealth-12-14.jpg)

How many assets do you need to be considered wealthy - this is a purely individual question and depends on the personal preferences of each person. However, when we talk about multiplying wealth, we definitely mean growth of wealth, some increase. So, let's consider important rules for multiplying wealth.

Rule 1. Save, don't spend. All expenses on personal needs do not contribute to the multiplication of a person's wealth, even if it's hundreds of thousands or millions of monetary units. Because wealth is what remains, not what is spent. Therefore, the first and most important rule for multiplying wealth is to not spend, but to save and accumulate as much of your income as possible. It is the saved money and material assets that form wealth. It is necessary to set aside at least 10% of your income, but the more portion of your income you save, the faster your wealth will multiply. People who successfully accumulate wealth usually save at least half of their income.

Rule 2. Money should work. If you simply save money without investing them, they will gradually depreciate due to inflation. Therefore, money should be invested in assets, preferably income-generating ones, which preserve finances from inflation and increase them. In other words, you need to invest. By saving money to create new money, you create your personal capital - the most important financial asset. Of course, it is also necessary to acquire spending assets for life, such as real estate and a car, but only considering the ability to maintain them without significant financial difficulties. These assets are also part of wealth. When investing, it is important to remember about risks and diversification. In simpler terms, "don't put all your eggs in one basket," but this is a very superficial judgment.

Rule 3. Pay yourself first! Where do you get the money to invest? It is important to save money immediately, following this important rule for multiplying wealth. If you save money at the end, according to the "remainder" principle, wealth accumulation will be much less effective, as the remainder will be small or even zero. It is very important to pay yourself first, creating wealth, and then others: sellers of goods and services that you acquire for your life needs.
