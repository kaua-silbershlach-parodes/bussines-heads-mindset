# Why your business could be losing money

![Why your business could be losing money](../images/053-Why-your-business-could-be-losing-money-04-13.jpg)

Just as all living organisms need water, businesses need money to thrive. And if a company is rapidly losing money, there is clearly something wrong with its business. The reasons why businesses lose money are:

**1. You are not keeping track of your expenses. **

If this point applies to you, don't feel bad - you'd be surprised how many companies don't keep track of their expenses for utilities, salaries and rent. For businesses, however, this can be a big problem, as at some point you may start spending more than you earn and not find out about it.

How to treat it: Gather all of your spending information for the last 6 months and start tracking your spending on a monthly basis. When you summarize at the end of the month, you'll be able to figure out what you can save money on.

**2. Your credit card debts have been outstanding for a long time. **

Credit card debt is serious business. High interest on debt will not contribute in any way to the success of your venture. Credit cards can be a useful tool for paying your business's immediate expenses and earning bonuses, but if you don't manage your debts on time, your business will start losing money extremely quickly.

How to treat it: Paying off credit card debt should be your number one priority. Start by increasing your monthly installments, and if the debts are too high, start seriously redirecting your budget in their direction.

**3. You don't have a rainy day fund. **

Having a rainy day fund is essential not only for businesses, but for almost all citizens. Unforeseen situations happen to everyone, and when it comes to business, it is possible that such a situation will deprive you of the opportunity to earn money to solve it.

How to treat: So that you don't have to make bad decisions in desperation if a force majeure does happen, your business needs an emergency fund. Make it so that a small portion of your profits are automatically deposited into a special account for emergencies.

**4. You don't bargain. **

Regular buyers rarely get to haggle over the price of a desired product or service, but in the B2B world, it happens all the time. If you're not haggling with potential service providers, vendors and partners, or getting special discounts and bonuses, you're simply giving money away for nothing.

How to treat: Always make a counteroffer. Never be afraid to ask a vendor for a 5% or 10% discount. If they turn you down, you're better off finding new partners anyway.

**5. You are jealous of your competitors.**

Has your friend's business recently moved to a luxurious office in a skyscraper? Are all your competitor's employees armed with the latest gadgets? Is the furniture in the contractor's office several times better than what is in your office? These are all understandable reasons to be jealous, but they don't mean your business is doing worse than your wasteful competitors. But if you start thoughtlessly spending money on such toys, your business may even become ill.

How to treat: Remember, always buy only what your business needs, not what you want. Used furniture and rental equipment can just as impress potential partners, investors or clients without leaving a hole in your pocket.

**6. Paydays make you nervous.**

After taxes, salaries are perhaps the main expense item for your business. If you are not sure whether you can pay an employee’s salary, then your company has serious financial problems, which may soon develop into legal problems.

How to treat: Almost every other aspect of your business should come second to payroll. Give up some assets, don't pay yourself, come up with new ways of financing, but pay your employees, otherwise your business could suffer very seriously.
