# How to properly present a project to an investor?

![How to properly present a project to an investor?](../images/044-How-to-properly-present-a-project-to-an-investor-01-15.jpg)

**1. Explain what you do.**

Talk about what you're doing as early as possible. Preferably in the first sentence.

**2. Get to the demo quickly**

Demonstrating a product will give you a better idea of it than anything you can say.

**3. A short description is better than a lengthy one.**

Your initial goal is not to talk about all future development opportunities, but to let investors know that you are worth talking to further.

**4. Don't do everything at once**

Have one person give the presentation while the other sits at the computer and switches slides. While you are standing in front of an audience, they will listen to you, at least out of politeness.

**5. Don’t talk for a long time about unimportant things**

If you only have a few minutes, spend them explaining what your product can do and why it's great.

**6. Don't go too deep into the business model.**

This is not what investors want to hear during a short presentation. Plus, the business model you have now is probably wrong.

**7. Speak slowly and clearly in front of an audience.**

If you feel like you're speaking too slowly, you're speaking at the right speed.

**8. Let only one speak**

Startup founders often like to emphasize that all partners in the company are equal. This is a good sign. Investors don't like it when teams are discordant. But there is no need to show this at the presentation, speaking in turns. It's distracting.

**9. Appear confident**

That is, you need to look confident, and not talk about it. There is no point in declaring that “we are firmly convinced” or “our product is incomparable.” If you've created something really good, then you honor investors by telling them about it.

**10. Don't try to appear better than you are.**

You just have to convince investors that you are smart and ready for something bigger. If you try too hard to hide your immaturity—for example, by trying to reason about things you don't know—you may be hiding your talents. Investors can easily spot a faker.

**11. Don't write long captions for slides**

If your slide captions are too long, people simply won't read them.

**12. Hard numbers look convincing.**

If you have specific data, even preliminary, share it with the public. The numbers are easy to remember. If you can state that on average each visitor views a page 12 times, state it. But you should not report more than 4-5 figures per presentation. And all the numbers should characterize your project directly. There is no need to talk about the size of the market in which you are going to work.

**13. Tell real user stories**

It's very helpful to talk about problems that specific users encountered and how you solved them. Or better yet, tell us how you yourself encountered an obstacle and got around it. You can also give examples of people you know personally - family or friends.

**14. Come up with a keyword phrase**

Write and learn a short key phrase that will stick in the heads of your listeners.
