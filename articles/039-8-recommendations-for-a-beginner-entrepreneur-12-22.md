# 8 recommendations for a beginner entrepreneur

![8 recommendations for a beginner entrepreneur](../images/039-8-recommendations-for-a-beginner-entrepreneur-12-22.jpg)

1. Preparation.

Preparing for business... It's a special state... When you understand that you desire this, or have decided that you want to work for yourself. When you won't work day and night for someone else... When you're ready to dedicate yourself to this cause... When you're ready to face challenges. When you live by the idea, when you breathe it - that's when you're ready...

2. Confidence.

Confidence in the idea. In your future business. What you want to open must be successful. Otherwise, it cannot be. When you confidently tell everyone interested, all employees, friends - YES, I will do this. YES, I will definitely do this. Only then take on the idea. If you're not ready to speak up, if you're not sure, don't even try. If there's an idea in your head accompanied by the words - I want or I think I will do this - that's not it. When you're passionate about the idea, when you believe in it - that's when you take it on.

3. Entrepreneurship.

Don't be a "worker". Be an entrepreneur. Organize the business. Lead people. They should see an entrepreneur in you. Feel it in your conversations and actions. See ideas, take everything necessary... Be visionary. Be entrepreneurial. Remember this.

4. Responsibility.

Business requires money. Understand that it's a huge risk. Don't take money without realizing that it's a huge responsibility! Many people have ended their lives because of debts. Relationships are ruined because of it. Friends are lost, loved ones turn away. If you take money - repay it. Understand that it's a huge responsibility and that there are huge risks! Don't borrow if you're not 100% sure about repayment. It's dangerous.

5. Knowledge.

You must know your field. Understand what it is and how it works. It's great if the company's field of activity is your passion. You know about it. You know the loopholes... Imagine a grandmother who wants to engage in web design instead of sewing production. It's not that simple.

6. Friends.

Don't hire friends. You'll realize your mistake as soon as you do. Friends don't fit into positions like hired employees. Friends won't handle it. Friends will want more than others. Friends can pressure you. Friends will drag your company down. You won't be able to fire them out of a sense of duty. If you hire a friend - you'll make a mistake. The old truth: "if you want to lose a friend, lend them money." Don't hire friends. You can collaborate with them, but don't put them on the payroll.

7. Honesty.

A good partner is reliable. Keep promises, don't let anyone down. Partners will stay with you. They will likely repay you in the same way. Don't deceive! Say - do. Keep your word! And people will come. But don't forget that in business, everyone can be cheated and deceived... Caution never hurts.

8. Economy.

Universities don't teach correctly! The perception is completely wrong! They impress upon us that a company is corporate culture, luxury renovation, new furniture, and fancy equipment! But that's not true! Save! There's no need for fancy equipment! Everything minimal! Why have offices for employees? Basements, Pentium 1, simple students working for peanuts. That's the reality of business. There won't be fancy companies. Everywhere, you need to save! Everything minimal!
