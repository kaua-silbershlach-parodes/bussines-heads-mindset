# 5 business principles that every entrepreneur should know

![5 business principles that every entrepreneur should know](../images/042-5-business-principles-that-every-entrepreneur-should-know-01-15.jpg)

**1. Value: what the client pays for.**

Business exists to create value of various kinds. It takes raw materials and labor resources and adds value to them in some way by transforming them into goods or services that are subsequently purchased by customers. Value is what customers pay for.

For example, McDonald's creates customer value by creating places where people can eat inexpensively while away from home. The company builds restaurants, hires chefs and people to serve customers, buys meat and prepares food. Customers appreciate the convenient location (no need to go home to eat) and the taste of the food (most people like hamburgers, chicken dishes, soft drinks and fries).

**2. Let's get organized.**

Any organization must have a good organization! It must have goals and resources (human, material and financial) to achieve these goals. The organization must track the implementation of all projects and the results obtained. Each department must perform its functions properly. Each employee must be assigned tasks that serve the goals of the organization.

**3. Competitive advantage: winner's wreath.**

To succeed in its market, a company must do something better than other players. Producing that something better creates a competitive advantage. That something can only be one aspect of a product or service as long as customers value that aspect highly. For example, a company can achieve a competitive advantage by offering the widest range of products. Or super low prices. Or high quality. Or great service. But you can't achieve all this at once.

**4. Control is something you should never lose.**

Once management decides how the company will create customer value, organize the business, and enhance competitive advantage, it must take control of the firm. This does not necessarily imply management with an iron fist (although some managers believe so). Rather, it means that everyone should be aware of the company's goals and given assignments that will move the company toward those goals.

**5. Profitability: you must have it.**

Business exists to make money. Money earned in a business can be counted in several ways. But no matter how they are calculated, a business must make money - make a profit from its operations.

If, over a period of time, a business makes more money from its activities than it spends on its activities, it is said to make a profit for that period. If not, he suffers losses. A company cannot sustain losses for a long time, otherwise it goes bankrupt.
