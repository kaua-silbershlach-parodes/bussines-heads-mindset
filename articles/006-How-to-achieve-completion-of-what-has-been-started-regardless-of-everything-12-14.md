# How to achieve completion of what has been started, regardless of everything

![How to achieve completion of what has been started, regardless of everything](../images/006-How-to-achieve-completion-of-what-has-been-started-regardless-of-everything-12-14.jpg)

**1. Make a to-do list.**

Take a pen and paper, open a special app or program, and right now make a list of all the things that are pressing on you. Take 10 minutes to relax, as you have already expressed everything that bothers you on paper/screen. Go back to the list, look at it critically: which of the listed reasons is preventing you from completing each of the mentioned tasks?

**2. Remove the unnecessary.**

Read the list again. Think about how necessary these macrame courses are, to which you were forced to enroll because of your bored friend? And will these books help you in your career? Delete tasks that are not feasible. Better spend time on something really important.

**3. Remember the "two-minute rule".**

In time management, there is a principle called the "two-minute rule": tasks that take no more than 2-5 minutes are done immediately and removed. Organizing a shelf in the closet, tidying up a shelf in the bathroom, loading the washing machine with laundry - all of this takes no more than 5 (maximum 10) minutes. Just half an hour has passed, and you have already accomplished 5-10 tasks!

**4. Become French - eat the "frog".**

Highlight in green all the items on the list that you haven't completed due to laziness. These are the most unpleasant tasks that you postpone until the last minute, in time management they are called "frogs". Deal with such unpleasant tasks better in the morning. For example, you need to write a commercial proposal to potential clients. Imagine in detail how you will write it, send it, and how potential clients become the most loyal ones. Feel it, do it, and then spend the rest of the day on enjoyable activities.

**5. Divide the "elephant".**

Complex and difficult tasks are called "elephants". If you don't know where to start, try dividing the task into several stages or subtasks. The simpler and faster a stage is completed, the better. What will your mind perceive easier: 5 difficult and time-consuming tasks or 20 simple tasks that take 5 minutes each?

The main rule is to come back to the tasks when you have a free minute. Suppose you have decided to learn French. In the morning, you learned one grammar rule, during the day - 10 words, in the evening you composed several sentences with new words according to the learned rule. Soon you will be able to communicate effectively with your French business partners.

**6. Praise yourself.**

There is nothing that can bring you more joy and inspiration than a long list of completed tasks. So, as soon as you finish a task, confidently cross it out and be sure to reward yourself for it. Your enthusiasm will increase if there is some form of reward for your efforts: shopping, a pleasant evening, a delicious treat with tea.

**7. Sum up.**

Set aside a few hours once a week or month (depending on your convenience) to summarize. You will notice that many tasks have been completed, a few "frogs" have disappeared, and a couple of "elephants" are still left undone. Once again, praise yourself and add new tasks to the list.
